Keep each claim's evidence; let an independent auditor judge it

A result can re-run and still mislead. The proposed work will keep claim-linked evidence while the research runs, so that an independent auditor can judge each claim. This is proposed research: none of it is evaluated yet.

SupportedContradictedInsufficient evidence

The gap: both runs reproduce; the comparison is still unfair

A system claims that its method beats the baseline. Re-running cannot see the problem below, because nothing fails. To see it, a reader needs the claim, the protocol, and what was actually run.

  • Both runs finish
  • The table reproduces
  • Different test splitsbaseline on split A, our method on split B

If the record of the split is missing, the honest answer is not “wrong”. It is insufficient evidence.

Illustration, from the proposal: “A faithful replay would reproduce an unfair comparison.”

The idea: every stage leaves a record

The unit of checking moves from the number to the claim. Each stage of the research will leave a record of what was really done, and each claim will point to the records and the sources it rests on.

In stage 4, a claim-level auditor, a second AI agent, will judge selected claims. It will also check that a cited source supports its sentence. It will return one of three judgments, with reasons and a next action, and the finding will go back to the research agent.

Independent means separated: a fresh session, its own workspace, read-only access. It does not mean that its errors are unrelated to the agent's.

The auditor is an instrument; the thesis asks two questions

The auditor is not the contribution. The first question is about assessment, the second about behaviour. An episode is one complete research run: a question, the experiments, and the claims at the end.

Same budget, three ways to use findings

A research agent will get a verified starting artifact and a comparative question, and run under three conditions with the same total budget.
